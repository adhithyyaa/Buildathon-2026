# Data Card — Overwatch synthetic training data

**There is no real customer or payment data in this repository.** Every model in `ml/` is
trained on a synthetic "world model". This card describes it honestly, including its limits.

## Purpose

A bootstrap dataset so the ML-first decision engine (recovery-probability, next-best-action,
escalation, anomaly) can be built, calibrated, and measured *before* real merchant recovery
outcomes exist. Real outcomes are the intended replacement and drop into the same feature
schema — the data flywheel described in [ADR-012](docs/DECISIONS.md).

## Source & generation

Generated by [`ml/src/worldmodel.py`](ml/src/worldmodel.py) — deterministic, `seed=7`, ~30,000
rows across 60 synthetic days:

- **Mechanism.** A per-reason base recoverability × a reason×action fit matrix, modulated by
  customer history, retry fatigue, opt-out, amount, staleness, and time-of-day effects
  (`success_prob(row, action)` is the world's ground-truth recovery mechanism).
- **Labels are deliberately NOISY.** `best_action` / `recovered` are drawn from *noisy* per-action
  probabilities (per-action multiplicative log-normal noise, σ≈0.22), not the clean argmax of a
  formula. This is on purpose: clean formulaic labels made an earlier model 99.5%-"accurate" at a
  tautology. Noisy labels make it a *real* learning problem (~70% action accuracy, ~84% agreement
  with the EV-optimal action). See the POSTMORTEM.
- **Injected incidents.** Concentrated 4-hour failure spikes per reason give the windowed anomaly
  detector ground-truth incident windows to find.

## Schema

21 shared case features (single source of truth: [`ml/src/features.py`](ml/src/features.py)) —
8 categorical (`currency, payment_method, failure_reason, customer_segment, merchant_type,
channel, last_action_type, last_action_outcome`) + 13 numeric (`amount` [log10 rupees],
`order_value` [raw rupees], `retry_count, time_since_failure_min, case_age_min, hour_of_day,
day_of_week, past_recovery_rate, historical_conversion_rate, prior_failed_attempts, opt_out_flag,
urgency_score, previous_contact_attempts`). The recovery head appends a candidate `action` column
→ 22. Labels: `recovered` (0/1), `best_action`, escalation, and per-window incident ground truth.

## Evaluation split

**Time-ordered**, not random: models train on earlier `day_index` days and are evaluated on the
latest ~20% of days (see `ml/src/train.py` and `ml/src/eval.py`), so reported metrics can't be
inflated by temporal leakage.

## Known limitations (stated plainly)

- **It is synthetic.** All metrics (`ml/metrics.json`, `ml/eval.json`) are on synthetic data.
- **The generator grades itself — mitigated by a second, independent world.** The counterfactual
  eval scores arms with the world's own mechanism, which risks flattering the model. Beyond noisy
  labels and the model-only-chooses/world-decides split, this is now checked against a **second,
  independently-authored generator** ([`ml/src/worldmodel2.py`](ml/src/worldmodel2.py)) with a
  *different causal structure* — see below. It is still synthetic (real merchant data remains the
  real fix), but the eval no longer rests on a single generator.

## Second world — `worldmodel2.py` (a robustness check, not a re-skin)

World A is **reason-dominated**: the best action is ~entirely a function of the failure reason, so a
reason→action lookup is near-optimal and ML has little to add (the eval honestly shows a tie).
World B is authored independently with a **different mechanism**: the best action is driven by a
**latent customer archetype** (how a person responds to recovery — retry-first, link-click,
reminder-nudge, discount-driven, hard-to-reach), modulated by amount and timing, **not** by the
failure reason. The archetype leaks into *observable* features (segment, historical conversion rate,
prior failures) so a model can infer it, while a reason-only rule cannot. Sanity check on the data:
`best_action` is predicted by a reason-mode lookup only **~24%** of the time, but by an
archetype-mode lookup **~85%** of the time. Same row schema → drop-in for `train.py`/`eval.py`
(`--world v2`). This lets us ask, honestly: *does the ML's edge over rules emerge when the optimal
action depends on context beyond the reason?* (see `ml/eval_v2.json` and Architecture §9).
- **No PII.** Customers are synthetic ids with coarse priors; no card numbers, PAN, CVV, emails, or
  phone numbers of real people exist anywhere.
